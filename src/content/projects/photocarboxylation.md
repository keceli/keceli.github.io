Using light to drive chemistry that is otherwise uphill—turning CO₂ into fuels and feedstocks, or forming new carbon–carbon bonds by photocarboxylation—requires photocatalysts that absorb visible light, transfer electrons efficiently, and survive many cycles. Designing them is an iterative conversation between synthesis, spectroscopy, and computation.

In collaboration with **Ksenija D. Glusac** (University of Illinois Chicago and Argonne), this project provides the computational side of that conversation: density-functional and time-dependent DFT calculations of ground and excited states, binding motifs of molecular catalysts anchored in metal–organic frameworks, and reaction energetics along photocarboxylation and CO₂-reduction pathways. The first published result is **Ru@MOF-808**, a Zr-MOF postsynthetically modified with a Ru-polypyridyl complex at one Ru center per node, whose photocatalytic CO₂-reduction activity was characterized jointly by experiment and theory.

The longer-term aim is to connect this work with the machine-learned potentials and agentic workflows developed elsewhere on this site, so that candidate photocatalysts can be screened computationally before they are made.
